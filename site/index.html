Thirty tabs, three worth reading.

Every feed hands you more than you can read. Deciding what to open is work, and you do it dozens of times a day with nothing to go on but a title.

Headlines lie. Thumbnails lie harder.

The title is written to be clicked, not to be accurate. The payload, if there is one, is usually buried past three paragraphs of throat-clearing.

You already know most of what you're about to read.

Most of what crosses your feed is a restatement of something you already understand. A tool that knows what you know can tell you when to move on.

How it works

Toss it in the air. Keep what falls.

  1. Verdict and confidenceRead now, skim, save, or skip. Confidence is the probability mass Jev put on that verdict; below 40% the pill shows “?” instead of a label.
  2. Insight densityA five-level rubric score mapped to 1–10. The display number is a convenience, not a measurement.
  3. Content typeOne of eight: original research, opinion, news, tutorial, listicle, rage bait, advertorial, entertainment. Rage bait and advertorials skip outright.
  4. Already knownProbability you already know this, given your goals, your recent topics, and what you have read or skipped.
  5. Claims supportedProbability the piece backs its claims. Opinion and news with unsupported claims get downgraded a step.
  6. Sales pitchProbability it is an undisclosed pitch. Above the threshold, it is a skip whatever else is true.
  7. AI-writtenProbability it reads as machine-generated. Only counts against low-density pieces.
  8. Payload and skip-toWhere the substance sits: intro, middle, end, or spread evenly. On videos with captions, the chip seeks the player to the segment holding the core idea.

Feed mode

Badges before you click.

On Hacker News, YouTube home and search, and any page with many external links, every link that scrolls into view gets a badge. Up to 12 links per model call. Hover for the full card. Verdicts are cached for 7 days, so a reload costs nothing.

      Typed answers, never prose

      Jev can't write. That's the point.

      Winnow never shows you anything a model wrote. Jev can't write. It can only pick — an option, a level, a probability. Every sentence on a card is a template we wrote, filled with numbers Jev returned.

      Jev is TypeSafe's “System One” decision model. It answers three kinds of question: yes/no as a probability, a choice among options with probabilities, or a score on a rubric, each with calibrated confidence. Winnow is pinned to jev-1.13.0, because the thresholds were tuned against it. More about Jev at typesafe.ai.

      Privacy by construction

      No backend. No analytics. No telemetry.

      The code is right there. You bring your own Jev key from console.typesafe.ai; it lives in chrome.storage.local, is read only by the service worker, is never logged, and never reaches a content script.

      Leaves your browser

      • The extracted page text or video transcript, capped at 24,000 characters, sent to api.typesafe.ai and nowhere else.
      • Feed items: title and snippet (domain, points, channel), or the linked page's text if you turn on prefetch.
      • A short summary of your reader state: goals, frequent topics, recently read and skipped titles.

      Never leaves

      • Your Jev API key, except as the bearer token on requests to api.typesafe.ai.
      • Your browsing history. Winnow judges the page you are on and the links you can see, nothing else.
      • Anything to us. There is no Winnow server, no crash reporting, no usage pings.
      • Anything from excluded sites: webmail, Google Docs, Sheets, Slides and Drive, Notion, localhost, and any non-https page.

      Permissions, and why

      • storage, unlimitedStorageYour key, settings, reader state, and a 7-day verdict cache of up to 3,000 entries.
      • https://api.typesafe.ai/*The only host the service worker talks to.
      • <all_urls> (optional)Requested only if you switch on “prefetch link text”, so feed badges can judge the linked page's body instead of its title.

      Tunable by design

      The rules are yours to move.

      Every threshold lives in one file, src/config.ts, and every one of them is a slider in options. The eval harness judges 40 fixtures with real Jev calls, tunes on 30, and reports on the other 10, so you can't fool yourself.

      FAQ

      Rough edges, disclosed.

      What about paywalls?

      The card judges whatever Readability can see. On a paywalled page that is the teaser, so the teaser gets judged as the article. Treat those verdicts as a read of the teaser.

      What about videos without captions?

      They are judged on title and description only, at snippet depth. Density, AI-written and claims rules are off at that depth, and there are no skip-to chips.

      What does it cost?

      Jev charges $0.042 per million input tokens; output is free. One Hacker News front page of 30 links is about 34,000 tokens, roughly $0.0015. A long article is 2,000–8,000 tokens. Everything is cached for 7 days, so revisits are free. Winnow itself is free and has no server.

      How accurate is it?

      On our 40-fixture set, split 30 to tune and 10 to report, Winnow agrees with the golden verdicts 80% of the time on the held-out 10. The goldens are still under review, and Jev 1.13 is an early access model. Expect rough edges: insight density saturates on well-written opinion, and content that argues for its own classification can move the answer.

      Does it send my data anywhere else?

      No. Page text goes to api.typesafe.ai with your key and nowhere else. There is no Winnow backend, no analytics, and no telemetry. Webmail, Google Docs, Notion, localhost and non-https pages are excluded by default.

      Does it work on Brave, Edge, or Arc?

      Any Chromium browser that supports Manifest V3 should work. Only Chrome is tested.

      Firefox?

      No. Winnow uses MV3 Chrome APIs.

      Can I change the questions?

      Yes. They live in src/jev/questions.ts, one spec per question ID. Adding one means adding its ID, a field on the answers type, the spec, and a parse line. The README walks through it.
